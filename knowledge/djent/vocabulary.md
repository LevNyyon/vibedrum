# Vocabulary: the request playbook

What a word means in modern metal and djent, the decisive default answer, what "more" and "less" change, and where the detail lives. Workflow: resolve the scope (section 1), look up the word (section 4) and the target (section 5), settle doubt with section 2, read the detail sections the row names (reading order by request type: README.md of this pack), write the script from his grid (section 6), and check it as section 3 says before you report.

Pointers: "grooves 4" = grooves.md section 4, "fills" = fills.md, "feel" = dynamics-and-feel.md, "song" = song-structure.md, `EP n` = rule n of knowledge/editing-principles.md (second version: 24 rules plus the acceptance tests). Every row and worked answer here obeys the EP rules, and where a line seems to differ the EP rule wins. Cells are grid=16 indexes 0-15 (beat 1 = cell 0, beat 3 = cell 8). `SEL` = the resolved selector, for example `bars=9-16`. A written digit is exact (8 = 112), a shown digit is a band 14 wide (9 = 119-127): read levels with `show --vel`. Ticks and ms come from the file's tempo and ppq, not from a table: ticks = ms x bpm x ppq / 60000, an eighth = ppq / 2 ticks = 30000 / bpm ms (feel 8, song 6). `T` = 5 ms in ticks: 5 at 130 bpm, 6 at 140, at 480 ppq. "unconfirmed" = working default of this pack, no source found.

## 1. Scope: what the words select

| he says | selector | watch for |
|---|---|---|
| fills, rolls, "my fills" | one group of lines per fill (`bars=4 beats=4-5`), each fill read from its own grid and ranked by fills 3. Never two fills on one line. The bulk `fills` selector only for a step with no shape in it (deleting ghosts), and only once every span in `# fills:` is confirmed and none is missing (fills 2, 9) | the header misses kick only fills, stops, stabs and short pickups, and wrongly flags tom grooves, ghost clusters and bars with a new blast or double time burst. A fill with a plain keeper hit after it, or in the last bar of the file, is still a fill |
| hats, hi hat | `lanes=42` closed, `lanes=46` open, `lanes=44` the foot. `lanes=hat` takes all three at once: name the pitch (EP 19) | a hat request covers the bars where 42 keeps time. A section kept by china or crash is named in the report, not edited. Only a song with no hat at all turns "the hat" into the keeper cymbal (feel 4) |
| cymbals | `lanes=cym` (crash, china, splash) plus `lanes=ride`. One voice: its pitch (`lanes=49`, `lanes=52`) | in a hat section "the cymbals" are the crashes and accents, not the hat |
| kick, feet, double bass | `lanes=36` | a kick on a `# riff` onset is locked, and with no riff track every groove kick is. Lock 85% or more: the riff owns the row, change velocities only. Where a kick may be added: section 3 rule 1 (grooves 1) |
| snare | backbeats `lanes=38 v=100-127`. `lanes=snare` also matches rim 37, snare2 40, ghosts and fill notes | ghosts and fill notes are not "the snare" unless he names them |
| ghost notes | `lanes=38 v=1-62`, with fill bars left out of `bars=` | 60 is a wall (feel 9, grooves 3): after any lift or spread clamp with `max=58` |
| toms | `lanes=tom`, one drum by pitch (`lanes=43`) | toms that repeat bar after bar are a tom groove, not fills (fills 2). Only tom lanes with notes in `# lanes` |
| a named section | `bars=A-B` of every occurrence | the mapping rules below |
| the whole song | drop `bars=` only when one recipe fits every section. Otherwise one group of ops per section, with that section's values | sections that return get the same edit |

- Bar numbers he gives win over everything else.
- "My fills", "the fills": every confirmed fill of the song. "The fill", "that fill": the one inside the bars last shown, played or edited, else the last fill before the section just discussed.
- "The chorus", "the verse": every occurrence. "First", "second", "last" pick one. "Into X", "before X", "the transition": the last bar before X (two bars if he says long) plus beat 1 of X. "The end of X": its last bar.
- "Here", "this part", "that riff": the bars of the last `show --bars`, `play --bars` or edit in this conversation. No such context: ask (section 2).
- "The intro", "the ending": first and last section. "The heavy part", "the quiet part", "the fast part", "the Meshuggah part": the wording column of song 9.
- A lane with no scope ("the hats"): every section where that lane keeps time, and no other section. A word with no target and no scope ("heavier"): the bars last discussed, else the whole song, on the word's default target (section 4).
- Scope never grows on its own (EP 7). A hat request leaves kick, snare and fills alone. A fill request leaves the groove bars alone, with one exception that is reported: the landing bar of a fill whose landing is missing (below).
- A section request may also touch the bar before the section (air, a build, the fill into it), its own cell 0 (the entrance) and cell 0 after it (the landing). Each change outside the section gets its words in the report (song 10). In every other section the keeper, the backbeat and the kick row stay.
- A later request reads the newest version: levels, H (the first note of a fill) and landings come from its `show --vel`, not from the original. A crash that a fill edit put on a phrase start takes the hat note of that cell, and after heavier fills a hat lift can climb higher (feel 6).

Cases the file decides, not the request (EP 4, 21 to 24). Read `# lanes` and the header before choosing a lever:

- Ceiling: a lane at min = max = 127 (sd 0) cannot be raised. `min=` and `set=127` do nothing there and "louder" is not a true report. Nothing is turned down to make room (EP 3): a keeper, kick or backbeat of his at 127 stays. The levers are weight and air (EP 4). "Heavier", "harder", "more aggressive" and "punchier" on a section with a china or crash keeper at 127 all take song 10 "the breakdown should hit harder" (the grooves 4 ceiling case points there too). Fills at the ceiling (119 or more) take fills 9 Heavier levers 1 and 3 (kick, air, landing): the ranks then differ by kick and air, not by peak, and the report says so.
- No riff track (no `# riff:` line in the header): the file holds no pitched track at all (the engine takes the busiest one by itself, `--riff N` only picks another), so there is no riff row and no lock column. Skip what only reads the riff or lock, take the "without a riff track" branch where a recipe has one, treat every groove kick as locked, and say so in a clause.
- A fill in the last bar of the file: confirmed, rank 3 when that bar also ends its section (fills 3 table). It has no landing bar: it runs to the bar line on its top, the landing is left out and the report says so (fills 6).
- Missing landing: beat 1 after a confirmed fill holds only a hat or ride hit, or no cymbal. A china or ridden crash keeper hit at digit 8 or 9 on that beat 1 is the landing. The fill requests for more or for another figure (Heavier, Busier, Longer, More tom, More interesting, Less cheesy) add it in the same script: one crash at digit 8 over the kick, the hat off that cell, reported. Lighter, Simpler, Shorter and requests that are not about fills mention it (fills 6).
- A kit with one or two toms: use only tom lanes with notes in `# lanes`. No drum leaves a fill, one drum more is allowed, the descent stays. The top moved onto the low drum (one `remap` of the fill's last tom note) is "heavier" asked again, never part of the default and never a whole lane (fills 2, fills 9 step 5).
- A recipe names a lane the kit has no notes on: the nearest lane with notes, same number of voices (EP 24). hh_open and hh_pedal count as the hat he has.
- Bars 1 to 8 in a recipe are a pattern (EP 20): map each bar by its role and look at what the mapped bar holds. A fill bar inside a range keeps its fill.
- Two sizes depend on the request, and both hold. Pickup top: 113 to 115 on a fill request (fills 3), 113 to 117 on a section request (song 10 fills rule). Air: the last 16th of a fill before its landing on a fill request (fills 6), an eighth before a section entrance on a section request (song 6, the fill slides). They never stack: a fill that already ends before the bar line has its air.

Section names to the engine's letters:

1. `# markers:` in the header names sections directly. Markers win.
2. No markers: a letter means one keeper plus one feel, not a song function (song 1). Give each letter a function with song 2 and song 9, then the position prior: intro, verse, pre chorus, chorus, verse, pre chorus, chorus, bridge or breakdown, chorus, outro.
3. Quick tells. Verse: hh or ride keeper, ghosts, lock 70%+. Chorus: the returning letter with the highest vel and the plainest snare row. Breakdown: china or crash on quarters, feel half, lock 90%+, rests in the kick row. Clean part: every lane at digits 3-5. No riff track: lock cannot be read, match the other tells and say so (song 9). A stiff file at 127 everywhere still matches on keeper, feel and kick shape.
4. A function can be a slice of a letter (a 1 or 2 bar build, drop or pre chorus never gets its own letter), and one function can span two letters. Always end at `bars=A-B`.
5. The same letter at two places is the same pattern returning: "the second verse" is the second range of the verse's letter.
6. Say the mapping in the report: "chorus = B, bars 9-16 and 25-32".

## 2. Ambiguity: default or ask

Act on the default and state it in one line when:

- the word has a default target in section 4 and the scope resolves with section 1;
- the only open point is how much: take the decisive default, the two or three levers with the most impact that have room in this file, in one script (EP 1, 2). He can say "more" or "less", and the row says what each changes;
- two readings differ in size, not in kind;
- the edit stays inside hands, velocities or one fill. Every version is a new file, so a wrong guess costs him one undo.

Ask exactly one question, two concrete options, the default first, when:

- "here" or "this part" has no context, or a section name fits two sections that need different edits ("the heavy part" with both a breakdown and a blast section);
- the request can only be met by breaking a rule of section 3: thinning or moving a locked kick, moving the backbeat without a feel word, removing a landing crash, cutting the tail of a fill;
- the word has two kinds of meaning and the grid does not decide: "drop" as a gap or as the breakdown, "tighter" when the hands are already plain and lock is 85%+ (levels, timing or kick placement);
- "more like" names a band this pack does not cover: ask for the one trait he wants (keeper, feel, kick style or fills).

Never ask what the grid answers: where the fills are, which bars the chorus is, the tempo, whether a riff track exists. Never ask a second question. After the answer, act.

The one line holds the interpretation, the scope and what stayed, and promises only what the diff will show (the Report test): "Heavier fills by weight: a kick under the fills that had none, the long fills up from their first note with the backbeat at full, each ending on its top, a crash for the bar 4 fill to land on. The last 16th of the fills in bars 4 and 8 goes for air. Groove bars untouched."

## 3. Do no harm

knowledge/editing-principles.md holds the general rules: decisive not exhaustive, levers by impact, direction, room, rank, landing hierarchy, scope, contour, a fill ends on its top, the heavy voice, never flatten, shape follows the music, walls, balance, limbs, order, sizes, a recipe is a pattern, file cases, acceptance tests. They are not repeated here. What follows is specific to this style, and no word overrides it:

1. Kick and riff lock (grooves 1). A kick on a `# riff` x cell is never deleted, moved, shifted, turned down or given `humanize time=`, and `lock` after the edit is not lower than before. A kick with no riff onset under it is still his: it stays unless he says "only the riff". With a riff track a kick is added only on a kick or weight request, and only (a) on a riff onset that has none, which is also the only way a backbeat gets a kick under it, or (b) for weight, under the strong strokes of a fill on eighth cells, where the riff rests through the fill's span (4 or more cells). No riff track: every groove kick counts as locked, and on a request for weight a kick is added in two places only: under a fill whose beats are empty where the bar it otherwise repeats has kicks (a hole), and under a backbeat with a kick on both cells beside it. The kick under a fill is part of the default "heavier", not a second request. A run or a carpet only on his word (grooves 6). Never a kick in the 1 to 3 cell rests of a riff figure or in the holes of a breakdown.
2. Polymeter. Where the kick row differs in every bar under identical hand rows, never `copy` one bar of kick over the others (whole blocks only). Keeper spacing and snare cell are the 4/4 anchor: constant inside a section. A keeper is never thinned in half a section, and never for "harder" or "heavier" (grooves 2, song 5 lever 2).
3. The backbeat. It stays on its cells (4 and 12, or 8) unless he used a feel word, stays in digits 8-9, is never turned down for a word that asks for more, gets no random timing beyond 2 ticks, and no ghost op reaches it: bound ghost ops with `v=1-62`. A fill that starts on the backbeat cell keeps that backbeat at the section's level, and its shaping ops start one cell later (`beats=3.25-5`). A fill level note on that cell (98 where the other bars have 127) goes back to full on a fill request for more (fills 5). On a section request the song 10 fills rule decides.
4. Landings. Beat 1 after a fill keeps the crash it has (or the china or crash keeper hit that is its landing) and its kick, at their level. A missing landing: section 1 says which requests add it and which mention it. The landing stands 10 or more over the fill's last note, or comes after air: on a fill request the last 16th of the fill goes and the top sits on the 8th cell before it (fills 6). A fill in the last bar of the file has no landing: say so. Never cut the tail of a fill. To end one early for the air of a section entrance: one eighth out of the drum with the most notes, the notes after it slide by minus ppq / 2 ticks (song 6). No drum with more than two notes: the last 16th out instead (fills 6). A fill you add gets a landing by rank (fills 6). A push does not remove the crash on the next beat 1.
5. Phrase and section starts. Crash + kick on cell 0 stays, and stays the biggest hit of its section. A two cymbal stack belongs to a section start only, never to a phrase start or a landing inside a section. Take it when the song itself opens on a stack, when he asks, or when the request is about that section's entrance or impact (song 4, EP 6). Never the next section's keeper before its first bar. The bar after a fill is the plain groove from beat 2 at the latest.
6. Untouched notes. Copy rows from `show` and change only the cells you mean: a kept character keeps its exact velocity and micro timing. Never rewrite a bar at a coarser grid than `show` printed. At a grid that does not divide the old one (triplets over sixteenths) `delete` the rewritten lanes first, or the old notes keep their ticks (grooves 7). A row of only `-` clears the lane in that bar. `copy` replaces the destination lanes: keep fill bars out of `to=`.
7. Selector hygiene. Every op carries `lanes=` and, unless the whole song is meant, `bars=` or `fills`. One voice = its pitch plus a `v=` band (EP 19): `lanes=snare` includes rim, ghosts and fill notes, `lanes=hat` the open hat and the foot, `lanes=ride` the bell.
8. Limbs (EP 15). At most two hand lanes on a cell, plus kick, plus hh_pedal. No keeper while both hands are on drums. No closed hat on a crash or china cell. No hh_pedal on a cell with a stick hat or in a beat with a kick run. Look one cell back: before a two cymbal stack the last sixteenth is empty or belongs to the kick, not to a floor tom, from 120 bpm up (fills 6).
9. One keeper per section. It changes only on a section boundary, marked with one crash1 + kick on cell 0, and by one rung per request (grooves 4).
10. Silence. Gaps before a drop, stops and the holes of a breakdown riff stay empty. "Busier" and "heavier" add at most one pickup hit there (fills 2).
11. Fill inventory and rank. At most one fill per 4 bars, no two identical in a section, none added inside a lock 85%+ section with a quarter note china or crash keeper unless he asks (there: unison hits on riff onsets at the section end). After any edit the ranks hold (fills 3): pickup under phrase end under section change, shown in the top, the body, the kick under the fill and the landing. A fill that starts on the backbeat cell peaks at that backbeat, so ranks 2 and 3 can share a peak: the step is then the kick and the length. No drum leaves a fill (one more is allowed) and a descent still descends (EP 8). Every fill ends on its top: the last note, a strong stroke, on the lowest drum the fill has (EP 9, fills 5).
12. Velocity bounds. Ghosts under 60 (`show` reads 60 or more as a backbeat, which can flip the feel label). Closed hat: shoulder 100 and up, tip 99 and down, 118 at most, spread `humanize vel=2` (feel 3). Fill body 118 and under, 119 to 127 on one or two notes of a fill (fills 5). Kick on the riff and backbeats live in digits 8-9: never spread them wider than `humanize vel=3`. The keeper cymbal bands (feel 1: china 104-124, open hat keeper 95-115, a phrase start higher) are a reading aid and the level of bars you write new: a keeper of his at 127 is never turned down on a request for more. No hand lane the edit shaped ends flat at one value, and nothing new sits flat at 127.
13. Order and once only (EP 16). Notes (bar blocks, `copy`, `remap`, `delete`, the slide of a fill with `shift`), level (the base `accent` over a whole part, `vel`), `humanize`, gestures (`accent` on single bars or cells, `vel add=`, `ramp scale=`, `set=` on one note, `ramp from= to=` only for the hat lift or a row written new and flat), wall clamps, every other `shift` last. A base `accent` after `humanize` erases the spread, a gesture before it is blurred. `humanize` and `shift` stack: one `vel=` run and one `time=` run per lane per section, and redo from the version before. The fill recipes and the song 10 recipes use no `humanize` (fills 5, song 10). A bar block for a `copy` destination goes after that `copy`.
14. Out of reach. Ops cannot change tempo or meter, and a bar in 7/8 has 14 cells at grid=16. Say what you did instead: "half time feel at the same bpm". `shift` has no bar line guard: never move a cell 0 note earlier (`beats=1.25-5`).
15. The file. Never overwrite his original. One new version per request, so every step can be undone.

The check before reporting is not a list of this document: run `vibedrum diff ORIGINAL RESULT` and `show RESULT --vel` on the changed bars, and go through the acceptance tests at the end of knowledge/editing-principles.md (Direction, Audible, Fills, Entrance, Not a loop, Scope, Report). A failed test means another script, applied to the same starting file. How three of them read in this style:

- Not a loop tests what the edit set out to shape: the keeper on a word that asks for life (alive, dynamic, less robotic, more human, groovier: the loop test of feel 6) and the groove bars of a section recipe (song 10). After a fill request or a level word (punchier, softer, tighter, smoother) a part that looped before still loops: a clause in the report, not a reason for gestures he did not ask for.
- Scope also means: with a riff track the lock column is not lower than before, the feel label is as intended, and `# fills:` still lists the fills you kept (new ghosts, a blast or a double time burst add candidates: reread it before any `fills` op). A `remap` and a slide show in `diff` as notes removed and added.
- Report: two or three lines read off the diff. What got louder, what came down, notes added and removed with their count, anything outside the named scope, what a file case skipped. A flaw that was in the file before and lies outside the request (a lane flat at 127, a missing landing) gets a few words, not a fix (grooves 10).

## 4. The words

Each row gives the decisive default: the two or three levers with the most impact, in order, all in one script (EP 1, 2). Not the safest lever alone and not the whole list. A lever with no room (already there, nothing to select, the lane at its ceiling) is skipped, never forced and never reported as done: the next one takes its place. "More" = the next lever of the row, one per request. "Less" = the smaller answer the row names, applied to the version before. It is never the opposite word. Direction (EP 3): a word for more (heavier, harder, bigger, punchier, more aggressive, build) leaves no note in scope quieter. A word for less (lighter, softer, smoother, breathe) leaves none louder. The life words (alive, dynamic, less robotic, more human, groovier) keep the section's average within 3 and say what came down. `N` in `add=N` = 127 minus the loudest note of that voice in the section. A row is a summary: the detail section it names holds the procedure and the worked answer, and the cells, levels and bars come from his grid (section 6).

## 6. Worked answers

A worked answer is one default answer on one example file. It is not a script: in both blind trials nearly every defect came from a block that was copied with new bar numbers. Use one like this (EP 20):

1. Read its `# input`, `# read` or list lines. They name the grid facts that every cell, level and bar of the script came from: where the kick is, which drums the kit has, how each fill is built and what follows it.
2. Write the same lines for his file from `show --vel`. They are the first comments of your script.
3. Decide each lever from your lines. Another file gives other cells, other bars and other levels, and sometimes no room for a lever at all. Never keep a block and swap the bar numbers, never put two fills on one line, never carry over a number that was read from the example's grid.
4. Apply, then check as the end of section 3 says.

Two answers live only here. Two idioms: `x` writes a new note at the lane's own level, so added kicks match the old ones. Mask: to delete notes by cell position, mark them with `accent` (digit 1 on the cells to drop, `-` elsewhere), then `delete ... v=1-20`.

"lock the kick to the guitar in the verse"

```vd
# said back: verse = bars 1-4. a kick under each guitar hit that had none in bars 1-3, 5 kicks added. no kick removed or moved, the fill in bar 4 is left alone
# input: riff track, lock 80%, keeper hh, feel normal. bar 4: beats 1-2 are locked already, beats 3-5 hold a fill and the riff rests there
# bar 1: riff onsets with no kick on cells 2 and 11. the kick on cell 4 has no riff onset: it is his and stays
bar 1 grid=16
# riff     |x-xx --x- x--x x-x-|
kick 36    |9-x9 9-9- 9--x 9-9-|
# bar 2: a riff onset with no kick on cell 9
bar 2 grid=16
# riff     |x--x --x- -xx- x---|
kick 36    |9--9 --9- -x9- 9-9-|
# bar 3 holds the riff and the kick row of bar 1 again. it still gets its own block: one kick bar is never copied over others
bar 3 grid=16
# riff     |x-xx --x- x--x x-x-|
kick 36    |9-x9 9-9- 9--x 9-9-|
```

What `diff` has to show: only kicks added (here 5), nothing removed, louder or moved, `# bars changed` = the bars you wrote, and `show` prints a higher lock column (here 80% to 100%). No `vel`, no `humanize`: old kicks keep their velocity and timing, new ones sit on the grid at the lane's own level. Another file has other riff cells without a kick, maybe none in some bars: list them bar by bar from its `# riff` rows. "More" ("only the riff", or asked again): the kicks with no guitar hit under them come out, here cell 4 in bars 1 and 3 and cell 14 in bar 2, counted in the report. "Less": only the bars he names.
