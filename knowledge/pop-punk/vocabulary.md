# Vocabulary: the request playbook (pop punk)

What a word means in pop punk, the default answer to it, what "more" and "less" change, and where the detail lives. Workflow: fix the notation (section 2), resolve the scope and the cases the file decides (section 1), look up the word (section 5) and the target (section 6), settle doubt with section 3, read the detail section the row names, write the script from his grid, then run the check of section 4 (`vibedrum diff` and the acceptance tests of the principles). The pack README holds the reading order per request type and the shared conventions: read it once.

Pointers: "grooves 4" = grooves.md section 4, "grooves P9" = its pattern P9, "fills" = fills.md, "feel" = dynamics-and-feel.md, "song" = song-structure.md, "EP 3" = rule 3 of knowledge/editing-principles.md. Cells are grid=16 indexes 0-15 (beat 1 = cell 0, beat 2 = 4, beat 3 = 8, beat 4 = 12, the "and" of 4 = 14). `SEL` = the resolved selector, for example `bars=9-16`. Cells, beat windows and ticks are for notation A at 180 bpm and 480 ppq: feel 1 converts them to notation B, the table of feel 7 rescales the ticks. "unconfirmed" = working default of this pack, no source found.

How to read a row of section 5 (EP 1, 2, 20). The default answer is the two or three levers with the most impact, in that order, in one script: not the safest one alone, not the whole list. A lever with no room in his file (already there, nothing to select, the lane at its ceiling) is skipped and named in the report, and the next lever takes its place. "More" is what he gets when he asks again, "less" takes the last lever back or names the mirror. A number in a row is a size, never a place: which bars, cells and drums it lands on is read from his grid, fill by fill and bar by bar.

## 1. Scope: what the words select

| he says | selector | watch for |
|---|---|---|
| fills, "my fills" | `bars=N beats=A-B` per confirmed fill, each read and written on its own (fills 3, 4, 10). Never the bulk `fills` selector for a level step: it gives every fill one treatment (EP 5). The landing cell (beat 1 of the next bar, or the push on 4.5) belongs to the fill | the header misses stops, stabs, two note pickups, flam pickups, quarter and 8th builds and silent bars, prints an 8th snare run as two spans, and wrongly flags floor tom verses and marching snare bridges (fills 3) |
| hats, hi hat | `lanes=42` closed, `lanes=46` open, `lanes=44` foot. `lanes=hat` takes all three (EP 19) | no section named: the bars where `hh` keeps time. Sections kept by a crash or the ride are left alone and named in the report. Only when he names a section whose keeper is not a hat, or the song has no hat, "the hat" is the keeper cymbal (feel 9, rows of feel 5) |
| cymbals | `lanes=cym` (crash, china, splash) plus `lanes=ride` | in a hat section "the cymbals" are the crash accents. A ridden crash and an accent share a lane and their bands touch: tell them apart by cell, not by `v=` (feel 2) |
| crash riding | the crash lane the `# sections` line names as keeper | accents inside the section go to the other crash pitch (feel 6) |
| kick | `lanes=kick` | base cells, push kicks and kicks on riff accents are not negotiable (section 4). No riff track: no groove kick moves (below) |
| snare | backbeats `lanes=38 v=100-127`. `lanes=snare` also matches rim 37, snare2 40, ghosts and every fill note | fill and build bars sit inside most `bars=` ranges: leave them out of a backbeat op |
| ghost notes | `lanes=38 v=1-59`, fill and build bars left out of `bars=` | always under 60. A plain fast beat has none: adding them is a Barker layer request (grooves 6) |
| toms | `lanes=tom`, one drum by pitch: `lanes=43` | floor tom 8ths in place of the hat are a tom verse (keeper none), not fills (grooves 2 row 7, fills 3) |
| a named section | `bars=A-B` of every occurrence, each read on its own bars (song 10, Scope) | naming rules below. The bar before it and the cell 0 after it are setup and landing: allowed, and named in the report |
| the whole song | one group of ops per section letter, with that section's values. No `bars=` only when one line fits every section | sections that return get the same kind of edit. A fill, build or push bar inside a range needs its own lines (EP 20) |

- Bar numbers he gives win over everything else.
- "My fills", "the fills": every confirmed fill of the scope, the song when none is named. "The fill", "that fill": the one inside the bars last shown, played or edited, else the last fill before the section just discussed.
- "The chorus", "the verse": every occurrence. "First", "second", "last" pick one. "Into X", "before X", "the transition": the last bar before X (two only when he says long) plus the landing on X. "The end of X": its last bar.
- "Here", "this part", "that bit": the bars of the last `show --bars`, `play --bars` or edit in this conversation. No such context: ask (section 3).
- A lane with no scope ("the hats"): every section where that lane keeps time. A word with no target and no scope: the bars last discussed, else the default of section 5.
- Scope never grows on its own (EP 7). A hat request leaves kick, snare and fills alone. A fill request leaves the groove bars alone, except the landing cell. A chorus request does not touch the verse: when the lift needs the verse lower, offer it in the report.
- Section names: `# markers:` win. With none, a letter is one keeper plus one feel, not a song function. Name the letters with the recogniser of song 9 (header fields first, then two grid features), the signatures of song 3 and the phrase rules of song 4. A function can be a slice of a letter or span two letters: always end at `bars=A-B`, and say the mapping in the report ("chorus = B, bars 17-32 and 49-64"). The same letter at two places is the same pattern returning. After a keeper edit the letters can change: `hh_open` prints as a keeper of its own.

Cases the file decides (EP 4, 21 to 24). Read them in the header before choosing a lever:

- Ceiling. `# lanes` shows keeper, kick or backbeat at min = max = 127 (song wide: confirm the section with `show --vel`). Stiff files of this style come that way. Those lanes cannot rise, and a request for more never turns them down: it takes weight (a second cymbal on the entrance, a kick under a fill, a lower drum), air (the last sixteenth before the entrance empty in every hand row and in the kick row) and the lanes with headroom (fills at 98 under a section at 127). Lowering the weak positions is contrast: only on his word or when he asks again, and reported as quieter (grooves 3 Ceiling, song 10 Direction).
- No riff track (no `# riff:` line in the header): skip every step that reads the riff or lock and say so. No groove kick is added, moved or deleted unless he asks for exactly that. Kicks are only added under fills. Pushes: only the bar he names.
- A fill in the last bar of the file has no landing: it ends the song on its top (117 or more), and the report says so.
- A confirmed fill with no cymbal on its landing cell: a fill request that is not for less gives it one crash at digit 8 with the kick, keeper hit off that cell, reported (fills 7). Other requests name it and leave it, unless their recipe puts a crash on that phrase start anyway (grooves 3, lever 3). In a section that rides the crash the keeper's own hit on beat 1 is the landing.
- A fill whose first note sits on a backbeat cell at fill level (stiff files have it): it rises to the backbeats only when the fill ends on 127 with air. In every other fill it is the first stroke of the fill. A real backbeat there (116 or more) is never lowered (fills 6).
- A lane with count 0 in `# lanes` may be silent in his kit: take the nearest lane with notes and keep the number of voices (lowest tom for the floor tom, crash1 for china or splash). A second crash for a section entrance may be written new on that one cell: say so.
- `show` folds a bar into `# bar N = bar M` only when drum rows and `# riff` row both match. Plain `show` compares velocity digits, so exact velocities can differ under a fold: use `show FILE --vel` when levels matter (grooves 1).

## 2. The tempo notation trap

The same fast beat is stored two ways. Notation A (fills.md calls it fast notation): 150-220 bpm, feel `normal`, snare on cells 4 and 12, eighth keeper on the even cells. Notation B (slow notation): 75-110 bpm, feel `double`, snare on cells 2 6 10 14, keeper on all 16 cells, one bar holding two bars of A. Test once per file, on the main verse or chorus: backbeats per minute (`normal` = bpm / 2, `double` = bpm, `half` = bpm / 4) sit at 75-110 for the fast beat in either notation, at 47-75 for a mid tempo song. Every recipe of this pack is written for A. In a B file halve the bar counts, convert positions (A cell c is B cell c/2, or 8 + c/2 for the second bar of a pair), write `pattern=86` where A has `8-6-`, halve every tick value, and read a `normal` section as the half time part. Never convert a file, and never "fix" a B file by moving its backbeats to 2 and 4 (grooves 1, fills 1, feel 1, song 1).

## 3. Ambiguity: default or ask

Act on the default and state it in one line when:

- the word has a default in section 5 and the scope resolves with section 1;
- the only open point is how much: take the default of the row, its two or three levers with the most impact (EP 1). He can say "more" or "less";
- two readings differ in size, not in kind;
- the edit stays inside hands, velocities or one fill. Every version is a new file, so a wrong guess costs him one undo.

Ask exactly one question, two concrete options, the default first, when:

- "here" or "this part" has no context, or a section name fits two ranges that need different edits;
- the request can only be met by breaking a rule of section 4: moving the backbeat with no feel word, filling the empty beat 1 after a push, removing a landing crash, taking the kick off the pushes;
- the word has two kinds of meaning and neither his sentence nor the grid decides: "drop" (drums out, or down to half time), "push" (anticipated hits, or playing ahead of the beat), "double time" on a section that already plays the fast beat (a short burst, or he means the tempo), "tighter" when the hats are already closed and narrow (levels, timing, or kick against the guitar);
- "more like" names a band this pack does not cover: ask for the one trait he wants (keeper, kick, fills or feel);
- the notation cannot be decided: backbeats per minute outside every band of section 2.

Never ask what the grid answers: where the fills are, which bars the chorus is, the tempo, the notation, whether a riff track exists. Never ask a second question. After the answer, act.

The one line holds the interpretation, the scope and what stays, in words the diff will show. Write it after reading his grid, for example: "Bigger fills, by rank: a kick under each, the roll into the chorus drops onto the floor tom and climbs to 117, the fill into the last chorus opens and ends on a snare + floor tom hit at 127 with a sixteenth of air, the pickups stay the small ones. Groove bars untouched, except the crash a pickup lacked." After the apply the report follows the diff, not this line.

## 4. Do no harm, and the check

Whatever the word was, an edit in this style never breaks these:

1. The backbeat. It stays on its cells (4 and 12, cell 8 in `half`, 2 6 10 14 in a B file) unless he used a feel word, stays a rimshot on pitch 38 at 116-127, takes no random timing beyond 2 ticks, and no ghost op reaches it: bound ghost ops with `v=1-59`, backbeat ops with `v=100-127`. The feel label after the edit is the one before it, unless a feel word asked for the change.
2. Kick on the pushes. Crash + kick on cell 14 with the next cell 0 empty is a push (grooves 5). Never delete or move that kick, never "repair" the empty beat 1 with a crash, a kick or a keeper hit, and a fill before a push lands on cell 14.
3. The kick row. The base cells of the feel (0 and 8 in the fast beat, 0 in half time) and the kicks on riff accents and stabs stay. No kick gets `shift` or `humanize time=`. Never add kicks to raise `lock`: 25-50% is correct under strummed eighths (grooves 4). No kick on every strum and no three adjacent sixteenth kicks at 150 bpm or more outside easycore (grooves 7).
4. Crash landings. The cell after every fill (beat 1 of the next bar, or the push) keeps its crash plus a kick. Shorten a fill from the front, never cut its tail. A fill you add gets a landing. No landing is bigger than the start of its own section, and two cymbals belong to the first cell of a section only (EP 6). Air, in this pack: the last sixteenth of the bar, or more, empty in every hand row and in the kick row.
5. Phrase and section starts. Crash1 + kick on cell 0 of a section stays, or sits on the push before it. It is the biggest hit of the section: 122-127, accents inside the section 10 under it. An entrance stands 10 or more over the last fill note before it, or has air before it. The keeper of the next section never sounds before its bar 1. The bar after a fill is the plain groove from beat 2 at the latest.
6. Untouched notes. Copy rows from `show` and change only the cells you mean: a kept character keeps its exact velocity and micro timing. Never write a bar at a coarser grid than `show` printed (a flam, a shuffle or a triplet bar prints at 12, 24, 32 or 48). A row of only `-` clears that lane in the bar. `copy` replaces the destination lanes: keep fill, build and push bars out of `to=`.
7. The lift. One keeper per section, changed only on bar 1 of a section. After the edit the chorus keeper is still at least one rung above the verse (grooves 3, song 5) and the last chorus is not below the first.
8. Limbs and speed. At most two hand lanes on a cell, plus kick, plus hh_pedal. No keeper hit under a crash, no stick hats inside a two handed fill, every open hat closed by the next hat hit or hh_pedal, no hh_pedal on a cell with a stick hat, no hand note on the sixteenth before a two cymbal entrance (EP 15). Eighth keepers need two levels above about 175 bpm and become quarters above about 210, and no hat sits on all 16 cells of an A bar (grooves 7, feel 4).
9. Silence. Stops, the silent bar before a chorus and the empty beat 1 after a push stay empty. "Bigger" and "busier" add at most a pickup in the last beat of a stop (fills 3).
10. The plain layer. No ghosts, no bark in every bar, no splash or cowbell in a fast chorus unless he asks for Barker or "busier" (grooves 6). No china, double kick or half time breakdown in a song that has none unless he asks for heavier or easycore.
11. Fills. At most one per 8 bars in a verse and one per 4 in a chorus, no two cell identical in a section, no finer grid than the tempo allows (fills 4). The rank holds: pickup under phrase end fill under section fill, 10 or more between their tops (EP 5). A fill ends on its top, keeps its drums and its descent, and its low drum is never the weak hand (EP 8, 9, 10).
12. Direction. A request for more leaves no note in its scope quieter, a request for less leaves none louder (EP 3). A flat part is shaped by raising its strong notes. `vel set=`, `vel min=` and `ramp from= to=` over a whole part erase its shape (EP 11, 18): they are for one named note, and for the hat lift of feel 6.
13. Velocity walls (EP 13). Ghosts under 60 (60 or more reads as a backbeat and can flip the feel label). Hat tip under 100, shoulder at 100 or more. Kick and backbeat stay narrow (sd 2-5 is the style): `humanize vel=3` at most on either, `vel=2` on a keeper, and `time=` only for "looser" and "more human" (feel 7).
14. Notation. Never convert a file between A and B. Ops cannot change tempo or meter: say what you did instead ("half time feel at the same bpm").
15. Selector hygiene (EP 19). Every op carries `lanes=` and, unless the whole song is meant, `bars=`. `lanes=snare` includes rim, ghosts and fill notes, `lanes=hat` includes the open hat and the foot, `lanes=ride` includes the bell.
16. Once only. `humanize` and `shift` stack: run each once per lane per section, and redo from the version before. Order (EP 16): notes, level, `humanize`, gestures, `shift` last. A bar block for a `copy` destination goes after that `copy`. Never overwrite his original: one new version per request.

The check, before every report. Run `vibedrum diff ORIGINAL RESULT` and `vibedrum show RESULT --vel` on the changed bars, then go through the acceptance tests at the end of knowledge/editing-principles.md with those numbers: direction, audible (a main gesture of 15 or more, or notes added or removed), fills (last within 5 of peak, 10 or more between ranks, no two alike), entrance, not a loop, scope, report. For this style also: the feel labels are as intended, `# fills` still lists the fills you kept, every push still has its empty beat 1. The loop test of feel 6 belongs to the words that ask for life (alive, less robotic, more dynamic) and to rows the script shaped: groove bars the script did not touch may fold as before, which is his loop and gets one line. A `remap` prints as notes removed on one lane and added on the other. A failed test means another script from the same starting file, never another wording. Report in two or three lines: what got louder, what got quieter, notes added and removed with counts, anything outside the named scope, what a case of section 1 skipped. No word the numbers do not show.
